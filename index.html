Hair treatments

01 • FUE

FUE Hair Transplantation

Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE) is a minimally invasive surgical hair-transplant technique. Individual follicular units are harvested from a donor area and placed into carefully planned recipient sites.

What happens

  • Hairline and density planning
  • Local anaesthesia and sterile preparation
  • Follicular-unit extraction
  • Recipient-site creation and implantation
  • Post-procedure monitoring and review

Important expectations

  • Growth is gradual, not immediate
  • Shedding can occur during the early phase
  • Results vary with donor supply and health
  • Follow-up helps assess healing and growth
  • Clinic assessment is essential before treatment
Before-and-after image supplied by the clinic.

FUE pre & post-care

Before

Follow clinician medication instructions. Avoid unapproved blood-thinning medicines or supplements only when your clinician advises. Arrive with a clean scalp and disclose allergies and medicines.

After

Follow washing and medication instructions. Avoid scratching grafts, strenuous exercise and direct sun exposure for the period advised by the clinic. Attend scheduled reviews.

Safety

Seek clinical advice for persistent bleeding, increasing pain, fever, pus, marked swelling or other unexpected symptoms.

02 • PRP

PRP for Hair

Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from a patient's own blood and injected into selected scalp areas. It may be used as part of a clinician-led plan for certain types of hair thinning.

  • Consultation and scalp assessment
  • Small blood draw and processing
  • Targeted scalp injections
  • Series and maintenance depend on response
Results vary by individual; images are not a guarantee of outcome.
03 • GFC

GFC for Hair

Growth Factor Concentrate (GFC) procedures use a concentrated preparation derived from the patient's blood. Protocols differ between clinics, so the exact preparation and evidence should be discussed with the treating clinician.

  • Scalp and hair-loss evaluation
  • Blood collection and preparation
  • Targeted application/injection
  • Response assessed over time
Results vary by individual; images are not a guarantee of outcome.
04 • LLLT

LLLT for Hair Fall

Low-level light therapy (LLLT) uses low-intensity light delivered by a device according to a prescribed schedule. It is non-invasive, and suitability depends on the cause and pattern of hair loss.

  • Device and wavelength selection
  • Regular treatment schedule
  • Protect eyes when required by device instructions
  • Stop and seek advice for unusual skin reaction
Diet: prioritise protein, iron-rich foods, vegetables, fruit and adequate calories; correct confirmed deficiencies with clinician guidance.
05 • SCALP CARE

Dandruff Treatment

Dandruff is commonly associated with scalp flaking and inflammation. Treatment depends on severity and may include medicated shampoos or other clinician-directed options.

Scalp routine

  • Use the recommended anti-dandruff shampoo as directed
  • Give the active ingredient enough contact time when instructed
  • Avoid aggressive scratching and very hot water
  • Keep combs, caps and pillowcases clean

Safety

  • Stop a product if it causes significant irritation
  • Seek review for severe redness, pain, crusting or hair loss
  • Persistent or recurrent scalp disease may need diagnosis

SKIN AESTHETICS

Skin treatments

Skin procedures should be selected after assessing skin type, active conditions, pigmentation pattern, medications and sun exposure.

06 • SKIN PRP

PRP for Face

Facial PRP uses the patient's own platelet-rich plasma and may be offered for skin rejuvenation. A clinician assesses whether the treatment is appropriate and explains expected results and risks.

Typical pathway

  1. Skin assessment and consent
  2. Blood collection and processing
  3. Topical anaesthetic where appropriate
  4. PRP application/injection
  5. After-care and review

After-care

Use gentle skincare, follow the clinic's instructions, avoid picking or rubbing treated areas and use sun protection. Temporary redness or swelling can occur.

07 • CHEMICAL PEEL

Chemical Peel for Acne & Pigmentation

A chemical peel applies a controlled chemical solution to the skin to produce a superficial or deeper exfoliating effect, depending on the formulation and indication.

Pre-care

  • Disclose retinoids, acne medicines and recent procedures
  • Follow clinician instructions about active skincare
  • Avoid picking, waxing or irritating products when advised

Post-care

  • Do not peel or pull flaking skin
  • Use gentle cleanser and moisturiser
  • Use broad-spectrum sunscreen and minimise sun exposure
  • Seek review for severe blistering, swelling or pain
08 • MEDIFACIAL

Medifacial for Different Skin Concerns

A clinician-guided facial protocol can be tailored for concerns such as acne-prone skin, hyperpigmentation, back acne and dullness. The exact steps and products should be selected according to skin condition and tolerance.

Acne careHyperpigmentationBack acneSkin glowHydration
Before

Share your skincare routine, allergies and recent treatments. Avoid unapproved active products if your clinician asks you to pause them.

Safety

Patch testing may be appropriate for selected products. Avoid treating broken or infected skin without medical advice.

Diet

Choose a balanced diet with vegetables, fruit, protein, whole grains and adequate hydration. Diet alone does not replace treatment for persistent acne or pigmentation.

09 • HAIR REMOVAL

Unwanted Hair Removal

Hair-removal options may include laser-based treatments or other clinic-selected methods. Suitability depends on skin tone, hair colour, body area, medicines and medical history.

Safety instructions

  • Tell the clinician about recent tanning and photosensitising medicines
  • Follow shaving and sun-exposure instructions
  • Use sunscreen and avoid unnecessary heat or friction after treatment
  • Report blistering, burns or persistent pigment changes promptly

Expected course

Hair reduction usually requires multiple sessions because hair follicles are in different growth phases. Results vary by body area and individual biology.

PATIENT GUIDE

General safety & nutrition

Safety first

Only trained clinical professionals should perform medical procedures. Tell the clinic about allergies, medicines, pregnancy, chronic conditions and previous reactions. Ask about expected benefits, alternatives, risks and costs before consenting.

Nutrition

Adequate protein, iron, zinc, vitamin-rich foods and overall energy intake support normal hair and skin health. Deficiencies should be assessed before taking high-dose supplements.

When to seek help

Contact a clinician for severe pain, spreading redness, fever, pus, significant bleeding, blistering, breathing difficulty or any rapidly worsening symptom after a procedure.
